## The McCandless Method and Diurnal Simulation

Dr. Stanley McCandless established a geometric system for illuminating acting areas in his 1932 text *A Method of Lighting the Stage*. The method breaks the stage into overlapping, uniform circular acting zones. Each zone is lit by a pair of front spotlights positioned at a vertical angle of 45° above the actor's face and separated horizontally by 45°.

This 45°-by-45° angle represents the diagonals of a theoretical cube centered on the performer. It avoids flat, feature-concealing shadows of eye-level direct lighting (0°), while preventing deep, unflattering eye-socket shadows cast by steep 90° overhead lights.

**Chromatic Contrast:** McCandless paired a warm-colored gel (pale straw or amber) on one front diagonal with a cool-colored gel (light blue or lavender) on the opposing diagonal. The shadows cast by the warm key light are partially filled by the cool light, and vice versa. This mimics natural light and models the human face for readability across large venues.

### Diurnal Transitions via Intensity Adjustment

| Time of Day | Warm/Cool Balance | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Midday | Equal intensity | Balanced shadows suggest neutral overhead sun |
| Late Afternoon to Evening | Cool higher than warm | Cooling light shifts mood into twilight |
| Full Sun Transition | Four fixtures per area (warm + cool on both sides) | Physically shifts warm "sun" from stage left to stage right |

## The Three-Point System and Side Lighting Dynamics

The three-point system expands McCandless by adding a **backlight** positioned directly behind and above the performer, typically at 45° to 90° vertical. The backlight outlines the performer's head and shoulders with a high-contrast rim, separating them from the background.

### Side Lighting for Dance and Physical Performance

Side light strikes the body horizontally at 0° to 90° relative to the audience, accentuating muscle movement and tracking lateral motion. Fixtures are mounted on vertical pipes (booms) in the wings, grouped by height:

| Position | Height | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Shinbusters (Shins) | Close to stage floor | Light dancers head to toe; create floating illusion against dark background |
| Mids | Knee to chest height | Catch midsections of performers; spill light across center line |
| Head-Highs | ~5 feet above floor | Direct horizontal side light to upper body and face |
| Lift-Lights | Above head-high | Illuminate dancers during lifts |
| High Sides | 45° to 90° from overhead battens | Simulate natural sunlight; keep stage floor clear of light |
