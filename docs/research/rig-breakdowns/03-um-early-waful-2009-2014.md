## Mantis Era (2009–2010)

Waful's first tour as UM's LD, supporting the *Mantis* album. He came to UM after moe. took an indefinite break. His design philosophy: "giant arena-sized looks in the tradition of Pink Floyd or Genesis."

### Verified Fixture Inventory

| Fixture | Count | Position | Notes |
|---------|-------|----------|-------|
| Martin MAC III Profile | 12 | 6 upstage truss, 6 on deck behind band | Core of the rig. Brand new fixture at the time. Waful: "the combinations are endless." Cone gobos, iris control, half colors. |
| Martin MAC 2000 Profile | 6 | Downstage truss | Wash band members. Three color-wheel gobos for audience house looks. |
| Varyscan P2 250 | 8 | Downstage or upstage truss, or deck | Small, lightweight, 110V plug-in. "Incredibly intense beam and very sharp gobos." Used flexibly depending on venue. |
| Martin Atomic 3000 | 4 | Floor | Used sparingly — "once or twice a night." Waful exercises strobe restraint even during frantic jams. |
| Barco/High End Fog units | 4 | Floor | Atmospheric effects. |

**Total moving lights:** 30 (12 MAC III + 6 MAC 2000 + 8 Varyscan + 4 Atomics)
**Vendor:** Performance Lighting (Chicago), Creative Stage Lighting (Northeast)

### Production Elements
- Large backdrop of *Mantis* album art, designed by Mark Blanchette
- Backdrop flipped to white for projection surface — MAC III gobos on white looked "like moving video"
- Pre-programmed with Hans Shoop: "A lot of the groundwork for my effects was laid by him"
- Waful wrote his first-ever complete cue list for the *Mantis* title track

### Key Waful Quotes from the Era
- "I almost always use a gobo since the lights are so bright"
- "I try to only use the strobes once or twice a night"
- "It's easy to use blues and indigos during quiet, pretty sections and reds and yellows for hot, aggressive rock moments. The challenge is how to get from point A to point B."
- "Some nights go really smoothly for me, and some nights do not, but that's what makes it so much fun."
- "There are moments when I might randomly decide that the whole band should be lit up in green to look like aliens, and it might be a magical moment, but other times I might guess wrong. That's the beauty of improv."

## MAC Viper Era (~2013–2014)

By ~2013-2014, the rig had grown. ECTO Productions (Chicago) became the new vendor, making its first lighting purchase — exclusively Martin fixtures.

### Rig Evolution
- **MAC Viper Profiles (1000W)** added. Waful's quote: "The Viper was the obvious choice. I find the sharp focus of the aerial gobos to be my favorite feature, in addition to the brightness of the beam and the lightweight body."
- MAC III Profiles remained as the core, on upstage truss and floor.
- Vipers positioned on midstage or downstage truss, occasional floor use.
- The Viper's compact size (81 lbs, 29 in) was a factor: "We play such different sized venues it really varies from night to night. We're happy with the convenience of the size and weight of the Viper based on how bright it is."

### Rig Philosophy
- The show core was built around MAC III Profiles on upstage truss and floor
- Vipers were the flexible addition — position changed per venue
- Still all-Martin fixture inventory under ECTO Productions

## Distinguishing Features (Early Waful Era)

- **All-Martin fixture inventory:** The early Waful rig was exclusively Martin Professional fixtures. This was unusual for a mid-tier jam band — Martin was premium-tier. ECTO Productions made its first purchase entirely from Martin.
- **Strobe restraint:** "Once or twice a night." This is notable — Waful was deliberately restrained with strobes during his first tours, even though UM's music gets frantic.
- **Gobo-heavy programming:** Waful "almost always" used gobos. The MAC III's glass gobos and the Viper's aerial gobos were central to his visual vocabulary.
- **Improvisational philosophy:** "Some nights go really smoothly, and some nights do not." Waful embraced the hit-or-miss nature of improv lighting from the start.
- **Hans Shoop's influence:** Waful credits his mentor Hans Shoop for teaching him MA programming and laying the groundwork for his effects. Many of Waful's effects still referenced Hans's original builds.
- **Cue list first:** The *Mantis* title track was the first time Waful wrote a complete cue list — the beginning of his hybrid structured/improvisational approach.
- **Venue flexibility:** The Varyscan P2 250s were chosen specifically because they run on 110V and work in any venue from clubs to theaters. Waful emphasized fixture portability and adaptability.

## RayFlow Relevance

1. **Mentor → programmer pipeline:** Hans Shoop → Jefferson Waful is a direct parallel to the AI → user relationship. Hans built the foundation; Waful programmed on top of it. RayFlow's AI should build the foundation; the user refines on top.

2. **Strobe economy is a design rule:** Waful's "once or twice a night" strobe restraint is encodable as an authoring constraint. The cue generator should enforce strobe budgeting.

3. **Gobo-first programming:** Waful's "almost always use a gobo" approach suggests the authoring system should prioritize gobo assignment — beam presets with gobo + zoom + focus combinations — as a default, with "open/no gobo" as the exception.

4. **Venue-flexible fixture choices:** The Varyscan P2 250's 110V capability was a deliberate choice. The rig builder should consider venue power constraints when suggesting fixtures.

5. **The first cue list:** Waful's first complete cue list was for the most intricate, composed song (*Mantis* title track). This suggests the authoring system should prioritize cue list generation for songs with known, composed structures — the easiest wins for AI-generated programming.
