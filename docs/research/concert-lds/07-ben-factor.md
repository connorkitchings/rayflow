# Ben Factor — Spafford, Umphrey's McGee

**Era:** 2010s – present
**Role:** Lighting Director, Spafford, Umphrey's McGee

## The Evolving Programmer

Ben Factor bridges two significant acts in the jam band scene: Spafford (where he developed his voice as an LD) and Umphrey's McGee (where he took over from Jefferson Waful in 2023). His approach blends deep busking infrastructure with strategically deployed timecoded sections.

## Design Philosophy

### Asymmetric Over Symmetric

The single most distinctive thing about Ben's approach — and what won him the Umphrey's job. He submitted an application rig using "groups of odd fixtures." Bob Stolz (production manager) flagged this as the differentiator: most applicants submitted designs that looked like Waful's symmetric style.

### Design as Continuous Process

*"I don't think there is a final form of like what the lighting rig or lighting design for Umphrey's will be moving forward, it's just a continuous process."* Iterated the New Year's rig across four nights: bars in X's → diamonds → spread-out diamonds → further spread.

### Experience Over Perfection

At the Caverns (subterranean venue, no rigging points, moat, slanted stage): *"The idea of symmetry just throw it out the window... make the design and make the placement of the lights more catered towards the experience as opposed to like a camera shot that's from perfect center."* Repurposed LED bars to face backward at cave rock walls.

### Design for the Space

For Red Rocks: wants to *"delegate a portion of the rig to specifically always be lighting up the rocks, cuz like that's the whole thing of Red Rocks."* For Summer Camp festival, designs a shared rig across four days of different bands.

## Programming Style

### Hybrid with a Busking Foundation

Factor's primary mode is busking — the console layout is designed for real-time performance. Umphrey's plays a different setlist every night with large improvisation sections. No pre-programmed per-song cue sequence.

### Pre-Production Workflow

1. Design the rig in MA2's built-in visualizer (can be done remotely on laptop)
2. Program palettes and cues against the virtual rig
3. Visit gear shop (Ecto Productions, near O'Hare) for physical prep
4. Load-in at venue (normally 3 hours: 10am–1pm for standard show)
5. Programming is ongoing between shows — continuously refines from laptop

### Application Process

Bob Stolz asked him to *"put a few songs into a 3D rendering and show us like what you would do."* Ben designed a scaled-down rig in MA2 visualizer and programmed lighting to music — essentially creating a pre-visualized show reel. Selected over thousands of applicants including Luke Stratton.

### Song-Section Mapping

**Core challenge:** *"They have a wildly enormous range of songs... and also within those songs there's enormous sections of improvisation. The challenge is how do I make a lighting rig morph and move and feel fresh throughout all these different fresh musical sections."*

- **Restraint → build → explode pattern:** Triple Wide opener — hold back on intro drum sample, full explosion on the drop. *"I'm not going full force during that intro drum sample, but then there's the drop where the whole band actually comes in... it's rippin' time."*
- **"Eat" = dark evil demonic metal:** Full strobe vocabulary for aggressive sections
- **"Remind Me" = strobe mania:** Heavily strobed dark sections
- **"Dark Brush" = heavy awesome:** Dark palette, high intensity
- **Soloist vs. ensemble:** Thinks about lighting Jake Cinninger solo vs. group improvisation — rig needs to represent group more than spotlighting individuals

## RayFlow Relevance

1. **Infrastructure inheritance:** RayFlow's show library should support importing another show's palette and sequence infrastructure as a starting point.
2. **Gradual voice development:** AI should support "voice development" — starting from a known template and iteratively customizing toward personal style.
3. **Pre-viz to music IS the RayFlow use case:** Ben got his job by designing a rig in MA2 visualizer and programming lighting to songs. RayFlow automates this for amateurs.
4. **Asymmetric fixture grouping as design personality:** Expose "personality sliders" (symmetric ↔ asymmetric, minimal ↔ maximal) for distinctive looks.
5. **Restraint → build → explode is automatable:** Maps to song structure: intro = hold back, build = increasing intensity, drop = full impact.
6. **Venue-aware rig design:** Ask about venue type and adjust fixture placement accordingly (cave = color wash on walls, arena = air shapes, Red Rocks = light the rocks).
7. **Busking vs. cueing continuum:** Understand where on the spectrum the music falls — jam bands need busking, structured pop can be more deterministic.
8. **Color vocabulary maps to mood:** "Dark evil demonic metal" = strobe/heavy, "psychedelic" = color wash. AI needs mood-to-palette mapping that's genre-aware.
9. **3-hour load-in constraint:** Generate rigs that are physically buildable, not just visually impressive.
10. **Continuous iteration is the norm:** Offer variation controls for iteration without starting over.
