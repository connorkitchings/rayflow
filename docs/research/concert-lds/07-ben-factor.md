# Ben Factor — Spafford, Umphrey's McGee

**Era:** 2010s – present
**Role:** Lighting Director, Spafford, Umphrey's McGee

## The Evolving Programmer

Ben Factor bridges two significant acts in the jam band scene: Spafford (where he developed his voice as an LD) and Umphrey's McGee (where he took over from Jefferson Waful in 2023). His approach blends deep busking infrastructure with strategically deployed timecoded sections.

## Programming Style

### Hybrid with a Busking Foundation
Factor's primary mode is busking — the console layout is designed for real-time performance. But he integrates timecoded elements for:
- **Complex song transitions:** When Umphrey's McGee segues between composed pieces, the transition lighting is pre-programmed for precision.
- **Signature moments:** Specific, identifiable lighting moments that fans expect at specific points in songs.
- **Video synchronization:** Umphrey's McGee uses integrated video content. Timecoded sections ensure lighting and video stay locked.

### Building on Waful's Foundation
Factor inherited Waful's infrastructure when he joined Umphrey's McGee — thousands of presets, hundreds of sequences, and a busking layout refined over two decades. His challenge: maintain the show's identity while evolving it with his own voice.

### Spafford Era
With Spafford, Factor developed his core busking vocabulary. Spafford's music — dense, improvisational, groove-heavy — demands an LD who can follow unpredictable jams. Factor's Spafford shows demonstrated his ability to build and sustain visual arcs through 20+ minute improvisations.

## RayFlow Relevance

1. **Infrastructure inheritance:** RayFlow's show library should support importing another show's palette and sequence infrastructure as a starting point — the digital equivalent of inheriting a predecessor's console file.
2. **Transition precision:** Factor's attention to song-to-song transitions reinforces the need for transition cues in the authoring system.
3. **Gradual voice development:** An AI authoring assistant should support an LD's "voice development" — starting from a known good template (Kuroda's approach, Waful's approach) and iteratively customizing toward a personal style.
