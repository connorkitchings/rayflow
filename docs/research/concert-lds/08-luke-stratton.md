# Luke Stratton

**Era:** 2010s – present
**Role:** Lighting Director, various jam and electronic acts
**Primary Acts:** Dopapod, and others on the jam/electronic festival circuit

## The Versatile Tour and Festival LD

Luke Stratton operates across the jam and electronic music spectrum, handling lighting for multiple acts on the touring and festival circuits. His work represents the practical, adaptable side of improvisational lighting — building effective shows under real-world constraints of time, budget, and rig variability.

## Programming Style

### Efficiency-First Busking
Stratton's layouts prioritize speed of deployment and ease of operation:
- **Streamlined executor count:** 8–10 faders rather than 15–20. Each fader does more.
- **Multi-function faders:** HTP/LTP doubling on every executor maximizes control points from limited physical interface.
- **Clear, unambiguous labeling:** Every button and fader is labeled for intuitive operation. If a guest LD takes over, they can understand the layout in 30 seconds.

### Festival Adaptability
At festivals, Stratton may have 15 minutes of changeover between acts, no soundcheck lighting time, and a rig he's never seen before. His approach:
- Arrive with a USB stick containing a base show file (palettes, effects, busking layout)
- Merge the base file with the house patch
- Begin the show within 60 seconds of the band starting
- Refine throughout the first song as he learns the rig's quirks

### Cross-Act Consistency
Working with multiple acts, Stratton maintains a personal "base file" — a show file containing his preferred palettes, effects, and busking layout architecture. When starting with a new act, he begins from this base and customizes to the act's specific aesthetic, rather than building from scratch.

## Custom Hardware: The LPC (Lighting Performance Controller)

**Source:** Rig rundown video (Boston run, ~2016)

Stratton built a custom controller called the **LPC full size**, designed to map 1:1 to a Grand MA full-size layout:

- **Three APC20 units** (Ableton controllers) repurposed as lighting surfaces
- Each APC20 maps to a section of the Grand MA fader wing (15+15 = 30 faders)
- **Grids above faders** map to MA touch screens (screens 2, 3, 4), with screen 1 being dynamic — changes with MA page changes
- **Programmable keyboard** with X-keys, trackball, and all standard MA programming keys
- Buttons can toggle between **executor mode** (show control) and **programming mode** (MA syntax shortcuts)

### Console Layout Architecture (4 pages)

**Page 1 — Colors:**
- Top row = full rig on any given color
- Rows below = same color with world filters (low spots, high spots, upstage, downstage)
- Dedicated gobo section for Robe points
- X4S pattern triggers (wipes, shapes)
- Flat prism trigger for beam looks
- Fader section: color rate master, specials per band member, symmetrical/asymmetrical pan-tilt effects

**Page 2 — Color Variations:**
- Same structural layout as page 1 but with shifted colors (e.g., ruby instead of magenta, burnt warmer reds, amber tones, CTO skin tones)
- Purpose: avoid repetition during long shows by switching pages to get "same vibe, different variation"

**Page 3 — Wipes:**
- Top = full rig wipe left-to-right
- Divided by fixture group (spots vs. wash) and direction (L→R, R→L)
- Playable color combos during wipes

**Page 4 — Effects & Song-Specific:**
- Zoom-in effect
- "Flipped" color combos (half-and-half rig looks)
- Room to grow for song-specific programming

### Second APC20 (Middle Section) — The Core:

**Positions (top grid):**
- Functional: down, blind-up, in, out, stage edge (double-tap = absolute versions)
- Creative: Low Fan, X Fan, Tri-X, OpenX, Pyramid, Irish Geometry, Halo, Halo 2, Teeth, Mountains, Ghost Ball, Crossbones, Two Stars
- Each position has **prism rotation baked in** — the prism auto-rotates to complement the position
- Cyc looks: direct (aimed at back wall), wide (maximum gobo throw), focused-to-one-point (all converge, then prism explodes)
- Band member positions (Eli, Rob, full band)

**Iris/Gobo Looks:**
- Tight → big → full wash on beam fixtures
- Zoom control per fixture group (beams and washes separately)
- Band locks (fix wash positions while moving beams)

**Strobes & Chases:**
- Color scroll strobes, wash strobes, beam strobes, white-everything strobes
- Chase section with rate fader: L, R, in, random, quarter-note, eighth-note, ultra-fast
- Mirror chases
- Color-based chases (shutter/dimmer replaced with color cycling)

### Third APC20 — Color Theory Grid:

Organized as a **warm-to-cool × bright-to-dark** matrix:
- Warm bright (magenta, raspberry) → cool dark (deep blue, dark purple)
- Named palettes: Electric Mayhem, Magic Hour, City Lights, Covenant, Toad Stool, Fireplace, Starburst Harvest, Dune (with frost), Hot Sun, Dr. Seuss's Jamboree, Carbonite, Jedi, Jedi Temple, Watermelon, Ocean Sun, Wario, Autumn, Avocado, Starbucks, Summer 94, Invader Zim, Pumpkin Spice, Cove, Dosio, Dagaba, Cloud World, Millennium Falcon, Rampancy, Aldderon, Pandora, Aurora, Wizard, Castaway, Alchemy, Cotton Candy, Subzero, Under the Sea, Electric Forest, Netrunner, Marshmallow, Fruity Pebbles, Fall 97, Moonrise, Transylvania, Cowabunga, Shahuli, Wonka, Rasta, Rainbow
- Stacks: Horror Flick/Scary Movie, Earthlike (Aldderon/Pandora/Aurora)

### Rate Masters & Tempo Sync:

- **Stop/Tap/Go workflow:** Stop zeros all rates, Tap captures tempo, Go copies tap tempo to fade times
- Produces **2-measure fades** synced to the music
- Tap half-time = 4 measures, double-time = 1 measure
- Separate rate masters for: color fade, chase rate, movement rate, position fade

### X-Keys Section:

- Blackouts, white/blackout wipes
- Bump buttons (hit in thirds)
- LED color bumps, white frost blowout
- Toggle between executor mode and programming mode (align, select, groups, pairs)
- Quick group selection buttons
- View switching for MA software screens

## Focus Workflow

Uses a **water bottle with gaff tape on a mic stand** as a focus target:
- Clear so you can see when you're ON it and when you're NOT
- All fixtures converge to one point — precision from every angle
- Replaces the older method of having a person stand downstage shouting corrections

## Design Philosophy (from the video)

> "As a younger designer I spent a lot of time like 'oh man if I could just get like a screenshot or picture of like Chris Kuroda's show file, that would be the thing.' But the reality is that you find your own way to do it and that's the way that you do it."

Key principles:
- Build for **playability** — the layout should feel like an instrument
- Use **world filters** to apply the same intent to sub-groups of the rig
- **Bake prism rotation into positions** so combos always look good
- Maintain **color variation pages** to fight visual fatigue over a 3-hour show
- Keep a **"glow sticks" look** as a reliable go-to (green beams, purple wash, blue front wash — evokes black lights)

## RayFlow Relevance

1. **Base file generation:** RayFlow should generate a portable "base file" — universal palettes, standard busking layout, genre-appropriate effect templates — that an LD can load onto any console and customize.
2. **Multi-act management:** The show library should support multiple shows sharing a base palette and sequence infrastructure, with per-act overrides.
3. **Chronometry awareness:** The authoring system should estimate time-to-deploy for generated busking infrastructure. Can the LD load this show and be operational in 60 seconds? If not, the infrastructure is too complex.
4. **Guest LD documentation:** Generated busking layouts should include clear labeling and documentation sufficient for an unfamiliar operator to understand the layout quickly.
5. **Color theory grid generation:** RayFlow could auto-generate a warm-to-cool × bright-to-dark color matrix from a vibe description, giving the user a navigable palette space rather than a flat list.
6. **World filter system:** When generating cues, RayFlow should support "apply this look to [full rig | low spots | high spots | upstage | downstage | band member]" — the same intent, different fixture subsets.
7. **Position + gobo + prism combos:** RayFlow should generate position presets that include baked-in beam attributes (gobo selection, prism rotation, zoom) so the user gets a complete "look" in one trigger, not three separate cues.
8. **Named evocative palettes:** AI-generated palettes should have memorable names tied to mood/imagery (not "Palette 7"). This helps non-technical users recall and request looks by name.
9. **Tempo-synced transitions:** RayFlow should support quantized fade times tied to BPM — "fade over 2 measures at 120 BPM" rather than raw seconds.

## LD Commentary: Dopapod "Braindead"

**Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DjuLCODuots (~9 min)

### Design Techniques Observed

- **Three "ghost colors" / "effect colors":** Three color palettes in the middle of his screen that get rewritten every time he fires a new look. All effect pages reference these three colors, so changing the look updates every effect that uses them. Decoupled color architecture.
- **Scrim lock:** Position lock on wash fixtures aimed at the backdrop/logo, so he can freely move the rest of the rig while those stay put.
- **Pre-load → reveal transitions:** Set fade time long → dial in next look silently → snap the fade fader down to reveal instantly.
- **Color stacks ("Scary Monster / Horror Flick"):** Single button cycles through a curated UV/red color sequence on each trigger.
- **Room-adaptive circle effect:** Symmetrical circle effect keys off four room presets (up/down/in/out) calibrated per venue.
- **Rhythm selection:** Deliberately chose kick drum as the rhythm source for bump cues — "sometimes you got to pick a rhythm, there's lots of rhythms."
- **CTO frost hold:** Warm white blowout triggered by a "PCS" button — intentionally warm instead of pure white.

### Song-Section Mapping

- Intro: red full rig → yellow low beams → slow prism fade
- Dynamic shift: cyan/blue wipe on specials, blackout rest of rig
- Mid-song groove: ocean waves movement, dark blue ambient
- Build: color bumps, high-speed gobo scroll, rainbow color scroll, shutter chases
- Peak: white strobe full rig, bouncing shutter, warm CTO frost hold
- Climax: big symmetrical circle effect, white + red scheme

## Rig Explanation Video

**Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kUqbuooHW28 (~9.5 min)

### Key Insights

- **Former software programmer** who mapped GrandMA2 onto custom MIDI controllers (APC20, APC40, Launchpad Mini, LPK25) — ~$600 total vs. $50K+ console
- **Color wheel ordering:** Palettes ordered to match the fixture's internal color wheel — adjacent buttons produce smooth transitions, distant buttons cause visible color wheel spin
- **Bright/dark × warm/cool grid:** Two-axis color matrix for "vibe-based" palette selection
- **Double-stacked positions:** Tap once = position A, tap twice = position B — doubles available looks
- **"Freak Out" button:** Single trigger firing color spin + prism + strobe simultaneously
- **Temporary color bumps:** Hold = override color, release = revert (momentary triggers)
- **Clone workflow:** Programming built for 6-fixture touring rig clones onto larger house rigs at bigger venues
- **6x Elation Platinum Spot 5R** — main workhorse movers
