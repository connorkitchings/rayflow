# Paul Hoffman

Paul Hoffman is the lighting designer and director for Widespread Panic, a role he has held since 2009 after three years as the band's crew chief, and he is president and co-founder (2005) of Pulse Lighting [1][2]. He tours with a mix of moving heads, LED washes and video, run from grandMA consoles [3][4].

## Verified facts

- Hoffman became Widespread Panic's lighting designer in 2009 after three years as crew chief. [2]
- He is president and owner of Pulse Lighting, co-founded in 2005, and toured with The Dead in 2004. [1]
- Pulse Lighting owns roughly 150 Ayrton Mistral and Ghibli fixtures plus grandMA consoles, motors and cables. [2]
- At MEMPHO (1-3 Oct 2021) he used 10 Ayrton Domino profiles on the stage floor behind the band for silhouettes, patterns, colour washes and beam-mode searchlight effects. [2]
- At MEMPHO he also turned the Dominos to face the back wall to project a star field during "Space Wrangler". [2]
- He called the Domino "by far the most powerful LED-based fixture I've used." [2]
- In spring 2015 the rig used two grandMA2 consoles and an MA VPU video server, with Hoffman controlling video from front of house. [3][4]
- The 2015 rig used Robe Pointes for beams (replacing Clay Paky Sharpy because they zoom to a wider beam), MAC Vipers for spots, GLP X4S for washes, and 1990s Vari*Lite VLMs. [3][4]
- GLP X4S units formed a tight matrix on towers behind the band to make intricate light patterns. [3][4]
- Hoffman said the rig "has to expand and contract without looking sparse or scrunched" so it works in small theatres and arenas. [3][4]
- To fit smaller venues he used straight trusses with slightly curved ends, fewer trusses and heavier fixture loading. [3][4]
- Each year's design is a refinement of the previous year's rather than a restart, and he cites architecture, sculpture and art as inspiration. [3][4]
- His stated goal: "We want to use as much of the production every night as we can." [4]
- In fall 2016 the rig used MAC Vipers, Bandit 5x5s and Robe 600s, with GLP X4 for front light and newly added Chauvet 6.9mm video panels. [5]
- Hoffman said he knows the music "beat-by-beat and note-by-note"; the setlist is often decided late, and he builds a different look each show, matching lighting mood to the music and triggering video alongside. [5]
- A podcast interview with Hoffman exists (The Light Side, host Luke Stratton), but its content was not retrieved. [6]

## Signature looks

- Colour washes and silhouettes: floor fixtures behind the band shine toward the audience so the musicians read as dark shapes against a bright backdrop. Tied to [2].
- Beam searchlights: tight beams sweeping from behind the band over the crowd. Tied to [2].
- A projected star field on the back wall during a spacey song. Tied to [2].
- Intricate patterns from a grid (matrix) of small LED lights on towers behind the band. Tied to [3][4].
- Looks that change with the song's mood, heavier or smoother, with video reinforcing the change. Tied to [5].
- Slow warm-to-cool colour evolution (inferred from [5] only in the sense of mood-matching; the specific colours are not sourced).

## Copyable on a small rig

1. Floor silhouettes from [2]: place the 4 moving heads on the floor behind the band, pointed at the audience with a saturated colour, to silhouette performers.
2. Searchlight beams from [2]: set the heads to a narrow, open-white or pale-colour beam and fan them slowly out over the crowd for a song's peak.
3. Backdrop projection idea from [2]: aim a head at the back wall with a dots or star-like gobo for a spacey song.
4. Matrix patterns from [3][4]: arrange the 8 LED washes in a row behind the band and chase or ripple intensity across them for pattern effects.
5. Mood-matched looks from [5]: build a few distinct looks (heavy, smooth) from your 4 heads + 8 washes and switch by song feel rather than running one look all night.
