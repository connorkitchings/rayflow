# Jefferson Waful

Jefferson Waful was lighting designer and director for Umphrey's McGee from 2008 until a health-forced retirement in August 2019 [1][4]. Before that he lit moe. and managed the band Uncle Sammy [2][3]. He is known for an improvisational, follow-the-band style on a Martin-based grandMA2 rig [1].

## Verified facts

- Waful has been the band's lighting designer and director since 2008 [1]; he first filled in at the Up North Festival, then took the job full time [2].
- He replaced longtime Umphrey's McGee LD Adam Budney and had previously been LD for moe. [5].
- He began his lighting work on a small LCD controller and Track Spots at moe., after trying out for that job at the 2004 Jammys [2].
- His stated first principle is "Learn the music"; he says knowing the songs matters more than technical training [2].
- He aims for fans not to notice the lights, follows individual instruments (for example strobes on a fast percussion line, slow circle effects tied to the bass), and avoids "busy" or "flashy" looks [2].
- His background is in video editing; he treats slow-moving lights during a triumphant moment like slow motion in video [1].
- He describes his style as intentionally simple, so it highlights the complexity of the band's music [1].
- In odd meters he marks each bar's downbeat with red and blue so the meter is easier to feel [1].
- He keeps fixtures evenly spaced and symmetrical, close to geometric shapes [1].
- The 2018 shows did not use timecode: the band changes songs nightly and tempos shift in improvisation, so he follows the band like playing an instrument [1].
- He rarely programs specific song sections; he programs cues for covers with iconic light shows (example given: Pink Floyd's "Comfortably Numb") as a tribute, and for original material relies on improvising in the moment [1].
- The band occasionally asks for specific cues (a drummer's blackout during one intro, a couple of cues requested by the singer); otherwise he has wide creative freedom [1].
- 2018 rig: 12 Martin MAC III Profiles, 6 MAC Vipers, 10 MAC Quantum Washes, 6 Claypaky B-Eye K20s, 5 base hazers, one grandMA2 console; vendor Ecto Productions, crew chief Aaron "Louie" Meyette [1].
- The MAC III Profile has been the rig centerpiece since 2009; he likes its wide lens for distinct split colors [1].
- Quantum Washes were used as downstage washes for the first time in 2018, replacing Auras he preferred less for brightness; B-Eyes debuted on the Denver New Year's Eve run and replaced Claypaky Sharpy beams because of truck space [1].
- In 2009 the band was among the first to tour the MAC III Profile; 12 were used, 6 flown upstage and 6 on the deck behind the band [5].
- Waful said that because the lights were brand new, "they don't look like other bands' lights" [5].
- He lit Phish's run in Mexico alongside Chris Kuroda and called it one of the highlights of his life [3].
- He directed the band's documentary "Reel To Real" [3].
- His final show was August 18, 2019, in Wilmington, NC; a back injury forced immediate retirement ahead of a planned end-of-2019 exit [4].
- Former crew chief Elliott Little filled in after his departure [4].
- A 4K picture-in-picture video of "Der Bluten Kat" at the Capitol Theatre, Port Chester NY, October 21 (year not stated in the article; published January 2018), was shot by videographer MKDevo, with a director's cut of commentary hosted by Luke Stratton [6].

## Signature looks

- Red and blue alternating on the downbeat in odd-meter songs, so a listener can see the count [1].
- Slow, restrained movement and simple, symmetrical fixture layouts that frame the band rather than compete with it [1].
- Effects matched to individual players: fast strobing or flashing for a busy drum part, slow circles for the bass line [2].
- Lights that feel "invisible": the audience feels the music more than it notices the rig [2].
- Big, bright gobo-and-color looks from MAC III Profiles with split colors across the beam (inferred from [1][5]).

## Copyable on a small rig

1. Odd-meter downbeat marking [1]: on the first beat of each bar, flash two wash groups red and blue, then return to the base color.
2. Instrument-matched effects [2]: assign slow circle movement on the 4 moving heads to the bass line and a chase or pulse on the LED washes to the drums.
3. Symmetry and spacing [1]: place the 4 heads and 8 washes in mirrored left/right groups so every look is balanced.
4. Restraint and simplicity [1][2]: build looks from one or two colors with slow movement, and keep busy chases for peaks.
5. Split colors [1]: give half the washes one color and half another across the stage for a two-tone look.
