# Andrew Goedde

Andrew Goedde is an Indianapolis-based lighting designer who has been the lighting designer and director for the band Goose since 2018, and who also owns a lighting rental company, Goedde Sound & Light [2][3]. He works with programmer Tony Caporale on a hybrid show file that combines cued song sections with live-busked ("punted") jams, run on grandMA3 [1][3]. (Not to be confused with Andrew Giffin, the Phish programmer; see `09-andrew-giffin.md`.)

## Verified facts

- Goedde has been Goose's LD since 2018, is based in Indianapolis, and owns a lighting rental business (Goedde Sound & Light). [2][3]
- He began when Goose played clubs for around 50 people, and described himself as "just kind of punting the whole show" in those early days. [1]
- He felt the lighting needed more structure as the band's sound developed, and brought in programmer Tony Caporale, who specialised in cue stacks and timecode. [1]
- The summer 2022 "Dripfield" tour cue-stacked 16 songs; the rest of the show stayed punted in a "jam band style." The show file mixes cued pages for song sections (verse, chorus, bridge, solos) with punt pages for jams. [3]
- The 2022 show used no timecode. Caporale used follow and BPM sequences to hold tempo. [3] For the fall 2022 tour he triggered BPM cueing through the grandMA command line so Goedde could focus on performing. [1]
- Goedde keeps a color look for every song and builds from there with the band, because Goose's improvisation is unpredictable. [1]
- Goose ran a full-size grandMA3 console on the 2022 co-headline tour; the Dripfield show was programmed on grandMA3 after three days of rehearsal in Pod 2 at Rock Lititz. [1][2]
- Dripfield was pre-visualised in Capture in Nashville. [2] Goosemas 2023 was pre-visualised in Depence 3 for a full week at Gateway Studios. [4][5]
- Dripfield rig: 16 Robe Tetra2 LED battens on upstage floor stands angled at 20 degrees, built by tech Danny McDonald. [2] The floor stands were part of the floor package. [2]
- Dripfield rig also had 18 Chauvet Color STRIKE M motorized strobe/washes, evenly split across upstage, mid and downstage trusses at 20 degrees, run in 96-channel mode with the faces pixel-mapped; Goedde: "They're as bright as it gets." [3]
- Goedde uses the Color STRIKE M LED tubes at selected moments for cool white light on the crowd. [3]
- Tetra2 looks include "digital symmetry," with opposite angles chasing in and out. [2]
- Goedde called it the first time Goose had "the same tour design for almost all venues." [3]
- Fall 2022 TAB/Goose tour: Goose's overhead had 16 Robe FORTEs and 16 Tarrantulas on two trusses, plus 16 Tetra2s on eight floor stands and eight FORTEs behind the riser line. Goedde co-designed with TAB's Marc Janowitz. [1]
- Goedde's first use of FORTEs (replacing Martin MAC Vipers): "had to keep the intensity down as they really punch through." Tarrantulas and Tetra2s were combined for flower effects. [1]
- The shows were webcast; no fixtures were dedicated to camera key light, and a camera feed at FOH let Goedde check his lighting. [1]
- Goosemas 2023 (Hampton Coliseum, four shows): six flown diamond-shaped truss "pods" at varied heights and depths, 42 FORTEs, 32 MegaPointes, 46 Tarrantulas, 60 TetraX, 16 LEDBeam 350s. [4][5]
- Each pod carried six FORTEs, six Tarrantulas as base wash, TetraX outlining its front truss sections, and a pixel strip underneath. [4][5]
- Goosemas had no video or IMAG, so lighting and scenery were the main visuals; each show opened with a timecoded intro tied to a "featured planet," new that year. [4][5]
- Goosemas control was grandMA3 over sACN on Cat5 with no DMX cables. [4][5]
- Goedde on FORTEs: "a fantastic bright fixture with a quality light output that looks brilliant live and on camera"; he valued the shuttering for framing the band. [4]

## Signature looks

- Rows of angled battens on the floor flaring in mirrored opposite directions, chasing in and out ("digital symmetry"). Tied to [2].
- Walls of bright strobe/wash heads that each show a pixel pattern across their faces, or flash together as a blinder. Tied to [3].
- Flower-shaped beam bursts when floor battens and overhead heads fire together. Tied to [1].
- A distinct base color per song, with the band's jams steered live on top. Tied to [1].
- For Goosemas, a ceiling of glowing diamond frames, each outlined in pixels, that read like hovering craft over the crowd. (inferred from [4][5])

## Copyable on a small rig

1. Hybrid file: cue-stack only the composed song sections and leave a few busk pages for jams. Source [3]. How-to: one cue list per song for verse/chorus, plus a separate page of free-play executors.
2. A base color look per song: save one color palette per song as the starting wash, then change from there. Source [1]. How-to: 8 LED washes get the song color; heads add contrast.
3. Mirrored symmetry: aim paired fixtures at opposite angles and chase them in and out. Source [2]. How-to: group left and right sides, apply the same movement with an inverted pan/tilt offset.
4. Pixel-style patterns on wash faces: split each LED wash into zones if the fixture mode allows it, and run a color chase across them. Source [3]. How-to: use the fixture's extended channel mode and a color chase over the zones.
5. BPM-based sequences instead of timecode: follow/BPM cues keep tempo with a tap. Sources [1][3]. How-to: set one master speed and trigger cues from a tap tempo or the command line.
