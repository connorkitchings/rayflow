# Saxton Waller

Saxton Waller is a Colorado-based lighting designer and operator who was STS9's lighting director from 1999 to 2017 [1][2], then returned to operating as Billy Strings' lighting designer in August 2025, replacing Roger Grant (spelled "Gant" in some trade coverage) [1][3]. His 2025 Billy Strings rig was a busked, rock-style design built around Robe moving heads and pixel-outlined diamond-shaped truss pods, supplied by Bandit Lites [3].

## Verified facts

- Waller worked with STS9 from 1999 to 2017 and helped build its light show [1].
- In January 2017 STS9 announced he was stepping away from its live shows "to pursue new creative opportunities"; his crew chief Tiberius Benson took over at the console [2].
- He grew up with some members of STS9 [2].
- He debuted as Billy Strings' lighting designer at the August 2025 tour-leg opener in Milwaukee (8/9/25) and then Minneapolis, replacing Roger Grant, who had lit Billy Strings from 2021 to 2025 [1].
- On social media he said the debut was the first show he had operated in almost nine years [1].
- Billy Strings' August 2025 livestreams on nugs were cited as a place to see his designs [1].
- For the fall 2025 US leg, Bandit Lites (Nashville) supplied the rig [3].
- The rig's core was 37 Robe iFORTEs and 26 Robe MegaPointes, plus nine more iFORTEs on the deck behind the band [3].
- The stage architecture was nine diamond-shaped pods of custom Tyler truss with projection skins: four flown high as mid stages and five upstage at a lower trim, outlined with a linear pixel product [3].
- Four flown LX trusses carried iFORTEs and MegaPointes spaced evenly across LX1 to LX3 [3].
- The brief was a "proper rock 'n' roll light show" with drama, contrast and energy, while keeping focus on the musicianship and adding mystery and anticipation [3].
- Because the band jams, the show is busked, with cue stacks always accessible and about 130 looks available [3].
- The iFORTEs and MegaPointes project gobos, colour and texture onto the diamond skins and backlight the band [3].
- Some looks aim all iFORTEs upstage with gobos; MegaPointes with frost filters light the band through large cyc-style looks [3].
- Before the tour Waller ran a shootout of about 16 beam and long-throw fixtures at Bandit's Nashville shop and chose fixtures he already knew well [3].
- He praised Robe's colour mixing for consistency and the colour wheel layout for putting "the right colours in the right places" [3].

## Signature looks

- Every moving head pointing upstage with gobo patterns, painting texture across the diamond-shaped screens behind the band (from fact: all iFORTEs upstage with gobos, projecting on pod skins [3]).
- A soft, even wall of colour behind the players, made by frosted beams spread into a cyc-like glow, with the band silhouetted or backlit (from the frost-filtered MegaPointe cyc looks and backlighting [3]).
- Glowing diamond outlines from the pixel tubes, giving the stage a defined geometric shape (from the pixel-outlined pods [3]).
- Big contrast between dark, mysterious moments and full-rig bursts (inferred from [3], which states the contrast and drama brief).

## Copyable on a small rig

1. Upstage gobo wash: aim all 4 moving heads at the back wall or a backdrop with a gobo and colour, held static while the band plays. Derived from [3]; record as one preset, recall as one look.
2. Frosted cyc glow: use the 8 LED washes aimed at a back wall or cloth at low intensity in one colour (add frost on a head if available) to make a wall of colour behind the band. Derived from [3]; one group, one colour palette.
3. Backlight the band: place heads or washes behind or above the players pointing toward the audience for rim light. Derived from [3]; one preset per colour.
4. Look library for busking: build and name roughly 20 to 30 looks (scaled down from ~130) and keep them on a page of buttons so any look is one press away. Derived from [3].
5. Dark-to-full contrast: keep a "mystery" look (one or two heads, low intensity) and a "full" look (all fixtures on), and cut between them on musical peaks. Derived from [3] (brief for drama, contrast, mystery).
