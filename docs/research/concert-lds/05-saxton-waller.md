# Saxton Waller — STS9, Billy Strings

**Era:** 2010s – present
**Role:** Lighting Director, STS9, Billy Strings

## The Cross-Genre Specialist

Saxton Waller is defined by versatility. He has lit electronic jam pioneers STS9 (Sound Tribe Sector 9) and bluegrass phenomenon Billy Strings — two acts at opposite ends of the musical spectrum. His ability to adapt improvisational busking across genres, rigs, and consoles makes him a model for the modern touring LD.

## Programming Style

### Genre-Adaptive Busking
- **STS9:** Electronic, instrumental, heavily improvised. Waller uses aggressive color palettes, fast chases, pixel-mapped effects, and movement generators synced to electronic beats. The lighting mirrors the synthetic, high-energy nature of the music.
- **Billy Strings:** Acoustic bluegrass at its core, with extended improvisational jams. Waller adapts to warm, natural palettes — amber, straw, lavender — with subtle intensity shifts and minimal movement during quiet passages. When Billy Strings peaks into psychedelic jams, Waller expands into bolder colors and movement, but always from a foundation of acoustic warmth.

### Rental Rig Mastery
Waller frequently works with rental rigs that change nightly. His skill is building an effective busking layout on unfamiliar consoles with unfamiliar fixtures in limited time (often during a single soundcheck). This requires:
- **Universal programming:** Palettes and effects that work on any fixture type. Avoid fixture-specific tricks.
- **Console fluency:** Deep knowledge across grandMA, Avolites, and Chamsys.
- **Efficiency:** Layout built in hours, not weeks. Every programming decision must justify its time cost.

### The "Rig-Agnostic" Approach
Waller's busking layouts prioritize universality:
- Position presets reference generic stage areas (DSC, USL, Audience Left), not specific fixtures
- Color presets use the broadest color-mixing channels available on the rig
- Effects use waveform types supported by all consoles (sine, sawtooth, square)

## RayFlow Relevance

1. **Rig-agnostic authoring:** Cues and presets should reference generic positions and fixture groups that map to any rig, not specific fixture types. This enables "tour file" portability.
2. **Genre-switching palettes:** The authoring system should support genre-specific palette sets — "electronic" (saturated, aggressive), "bluegrass/acoustic" (warm, natural), "rock" (balanced, versatile).
3. **Efficiency-weighted authoring:** When generating busking infrastructure, prioritize the highest-impact elements first. The LD may only have 2 hours to build a layout. What absolutely must be there?
4. **Multi-console export:** RayFlow's export system should eventually target grandMA, Chamsys, and Avolites — the consoles Waller (and most touring LDs) encounter on rental rigs.
